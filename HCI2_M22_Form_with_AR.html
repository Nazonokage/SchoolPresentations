Topic 1 · The HCI & AR Connection
The Symbiosis of HCI and AR
HCI and AR are collaborative fields — HCI ensures AR is user-friendly, and AR gives HCI new immersive platforms to explore.
🌟 What is Augmented Reality?
AR enhances the physical world with digital information. By combining the physical and the virtual, it has the potential to completely revolutionize how we interact with computers.
👤 What is Human-Computer Interaction?
HCI is the field dedicated to studying how people interact with technology. It focuses on the needs and capabilities of human users so we can design technologies that are intuitive, easy to use, and accessible.
🤝 Why HCI Matters for AR
Applying HCI principles to AR design ensures that virtual elements feel like a natural, seamless extension of the real world — rather than a clunky, separate layer bolted on top.

In the case of AR, HCI plays an absolutely crucial role — without it, AR interfaces feel disorienting, awkward, or exhausting to use.
Topic 1 · How AR Enhances HCI
Three Ways AR Transforms HCI
When AR is applied to HCI, it profoundly improves the user experience in three key areas.
🌌
1. Immersive & Interactive Experience
AR lets users engage with digital content directly in their real-world context. Instead of clicking a mouse, users interact using:
  • ▸Hands & natural gestures
  • ▸Body movements
  • ▸Vocal commands
♿
2. Accessibility & Usability
AR makes interfaces more accessible — especially for individuals with disabilities or limited mobility:
  • ▸Visual/audio cues for tasks
  • ▸Hands-free voice control
  • ▸Complex task guidance
🎓
3. Creativity & Learning
AR provides a vibrant new platform for education and creative exploration. Users can:
  • ▸Interact with edu games
  • ▸Run 3D simulations
  • ▸Hands-on problem-solving
💡 Ultimately, applying AR technologies to HCI allows us to create innovative, intuitive, and engaging experiences that enrich our daily lives — far beyond what traditional keyboards and mice can offer.

Skill-Building · Steps 1–3
Build the AR & QR Assets
1
Create and Publish an AR Experience
Use a tool like Ar.js or an online WebAR builder to create your AR experience. Ensure it is published and available online — host it on a website or via cloud storage like Google Drive or Dropbox.
2
Generate a URL-Based QR Code
Open a QR code generator app on your phone or computer:
  • ▸Paste the URL of the AR experience from Step 1
  • ▸Generate the QR code image
  • ▸This QR code acts as a bridge that directs users to your AR content
3
Open Your Target Google Form
Open the Google Form you want to link to this AR experience. This will be the form users fill out after they interact with your AR. It collects their responses, reflections, or answers.
💡 Tip: Recall M11 (QR Code Generator) — the JavaScript QRious library you built there generates exactly this kind of URL-based QR code. That project was preparation for this workflow.
Skill-Building · Steps 4–6
Assemble the Google Form
4
Add a Scanning Question to the Form
In your Google Form, add a new question that prompts the user to scan the QR code. Use a standard text field or a multiple-choice question so they acknowledge this step before continuing.
5
Insert the QR Code Image
Upload or link the QR code image you generated in Step 2 into the form. Do this by using the “Insert Image” tool in the Google Forms editor, or by adding an image field to the form.
6
Publish and Share
Publish the Google Form and share the link with your intended audience.

When users open the form, they will see the QR code. By scanning it with their phone or tablet, they will be instantly transported to your AR experience — then return to complete the rest of the form.
🔍 Test the Flow: Armed with your phone and a QR scanner app, you can test this entire pipeline in minutes. Try scanning a sample form like https://forms.gle/m5Af8unCBj8UnyYU7 to see how it feels from the user's side.
Bonus · Programmatic QR Generation
Generating QR Codes with Python
Prefer to generate QR codes in code instead of a web app? Use the popular qrcode Python library. First install: pip install qrcode
python — generate_qr.py
import qrcode

# 1. Create the QR code object with specific settings
qr = qrcode.QRCode(
    version=1,
    error_correction=qrcode.constants.ERROR_CORRECT_L,
    box_size=10,
    border=4,
)

# 2. Add the URL to your AR experience (Replace with your actual URL)
ar_experience_url = "https://www.example.com/ar-experience"
qr.add_data(ar_experience_url)
qr.make(fit=True)

# 3. Create an image from the QR code data
img = qr.make_image(fill_color="black", back_color="white")

# 4. Save the image to a local file
img.save("qr-code.png")

print("QR Code successfully generated and saved as qr-code.png!")
📌 QRCode() Parameters
  • version=1 — matrix size
  • box_size=10 — pixels per box
  • border=4 — white margin size
  • error_correction — damage tolerance
🎨 Colors are Customizable
Change fill_color and back_color to any HTML/CSS color:

fill_color="blue"
back_color="yellow"
🔗 Change the URL
Replace ar_experience_url with your actual AR page URL — that's the only line you truly must edit for your own project.

FAQs
Common Questions Answered
❓ What are the two main forms of Augmented Reality?
AR is broadly classified into two groups:
  • Marker-Based AR — the primitive version. Relies on scanning specific physical images or patterns (like QR codes) to trigger digital content
  • Markerless AR — uses GPS, compass, and surface detection to place objects anywhere in the real world without a specific visual trigger
❓ What are the 3 core elements of Augmented Reality?
To be considered true AR, a system must incorporate:
  1. 1.A combination of digital and physical worlds
  2. 2.Interactions made in real-time
  3. 3.Accurate 3D identification (registration) of virtual and real objects
❓ Why use a Google Form for AR instead of a mobile app?
Using a web-based link (Google Form + WebAR) is much more accessible:
  • Users don't need to download a heavy app
  • They simply scan with their built-in camera app
  • Google Forms automatically collect data
  • It's a low-cost, zero-code way to track user interactions
❓ Marker-Based vs Markerless AR — What's the Difference?
Marker-Based AR requires the camera to see a specific target image (printed QR code or picture) to anchor the digital object. Markerless AR uses the phone's GPS, compass, and surface detection to place objects anywhere — no specific visual trigger needed.

Multiple Correct Answers
Which of the following statements are TRUE based on the code above?
  • A.The generated QR code will be saved in black and white.
  • B.The QR code will point to the URL https://www.google.com.
  • C.The fill_color and back_color parameters can be changed to create colored QR codes.
  • D.This code uses the pyttsx3 library to generate audio.
Answer Key · Code Analysis
Correct Answers — A and C
A ✅
TRUE — The QR code will be saved in black and white
The script calls make_image(fill_color="black", back_color="white"), explicitly setting the fill (foreground) to black and the background to white. So the output PNG is definitely black and white.
B ❌
FALSE — The QR does NOT point to google.com
The URL passed inside qr.add_data() is 'https://www.ar-example.com/view' — not google.com. When users scan the code, they will be taken to the AR example URL only.
C ✅
TRUE — fill_color and back_color are customizable
Both parameters accept any valid CSS/HTML color string (e.g., "blue", "#ff0000", "yellow"). Changing them will produce colored QR codes — useful for branding, marketing, or design.
D ❌
FALSE — No pyttsx3, no audio generation
The only library imported is qrcode. There is no import pyttsx3, no audio functions, no .mp3 output. This code generates a PNG image file — not audio.
