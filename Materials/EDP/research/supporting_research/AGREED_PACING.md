# Current pacing decision: 17 modules per subject

Updated 2 November 2026. This decision supersedes the earlier 23-module target. The current phase remains research and planning.

## Period allocation

| Period | Module numbers | Count | Purpose |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| Prelim | 01–04 | 4 | Engaging opening and programming refresher |
| Midterm | 05–09 | 5 | Subject foundations and guided applications |
| Pre-final | 10–15 | 6 | Data/async behavior, reliability, and integration |
| Final | 16–17 | 2 | Review, troubleshooting, and individual practical-project preparation |
| **Each subject** | **01–17** | **17** | **34 planned modules across ADET and EDP** |

This is a target inventory with scheduling flexibility, not a guarantee of 17 available teaching days. The user specifically requested 17 in case time permits. Summatives and periodical exams remain separate from the module count. Saturdays remain excluded; each subject has two meetings weekly.

## Opening and Prelim foundations

Use the first module's opening for brief introductions and a compelling demonstration. Ask students to share their name, an app they use, and one small campus inconvenience they would like to solve. The teacher then demonstrates a tiny program connected to that problem and invites students to predict what will change.

- ADET opening: preview a simple Expo campus helper or baon app. Students are not expected to understand React yet.
- EDP opening: preview a button-driven counter or order board; ask what makes the program respond.

Suggested first-meeting timing: 10 minutes introductions, 10 minutes course preview/demo, 10 minutes explaining variables and values, 20 minutes guided calculation, and 10 minutes modification/recap. A 90-minute meeting can add supported practice. Use a teacher-prepared console environment so software installation does not consume the whole opening.

Suggested Prelim research sequence for both subjects:

1. **Meet the course; variables, constants, values, and simple output.** A baon calculation provides a first success.
2. **Conditionals and decisions.** Compare an amount with a budget and test boundary cases.
3. **Loops and repeated work.** Trace a short repeated calculation and its stopping condition.
4. **Functions, parameters, and return values.** Turn a familiar calculation into a reusable function.

Basic operators and data types are introduced alongside the calculations. Arrays/objects and the TypeScript bridge follow these foundations; the later subject plans still need resequencing to fit the revised count. Do not retain the older 23-module numbering as though it were the final sequence.

If the institution uses the first entire meeting only for orientation, record it as a separate orientation day. That consumes a calendar slot and requires moving M01 to the next available teaching day; it is not a free additional meeting. Do not quietly remove the programming refresher to compensate.

## Flexibility when time is short

Pre-final has the most demanding allocation. Design M15 as a familiar integration/rehearsal activity that does not introduce prerequisites needed by Finals. If a teaching day is lost, the instructor can retain that activity as optional practice after reviewing the actual timetable. The prepared inventory can remain at 17 while the delivered sequence is recorded honestly.

Protect the four Prelim foundation modules, the separate assessments, and the two Finals preparation modules. Introduce individual project planning before Finals. Advanced workers, live cloud AI, full real-time servers, store publication, and extensive device integrations remain candidate extensions rather than mandatory requirements.

The existing 23-topic outlines are retained as source inventories. Their useful content will be selected and reorganized into the 17-module sequence for each subject before lesson authoring.
