# Principles of Good Website Design

---

## A. Lesson Preview & Review

Welcome to Module 13! In the previous lesson, we focused on the methodology of Task-Centered Design. Now, we are going to look at the actual **Principles of Good Website Design**.

An effective website must fulfill its intended function. It needs to convey a message while simultaneously engaging the visitor. Several factors contribute to this: consistency, colors, typography, imagery, simplicity, and functionality. 

> **In a Nutshell:** A well-designed website builds trust and guides visitors to take action. Creating a great user experience (UX) means optimizing your site for both **form** (aesthetics) and **functionality** (how easy it is to use).

**Learning Objectives:**
By the end of this module, you should be able to:
1. Identify the 9 principles of good web design.
2. Understand how good web design affects the user.
3. Create a sample good web design plan.

## B. Main Lesson: The 9 Principles

### 1. Website Purpose
Your website must accommodate the needs of the user. Having a simple, clear intention on all pages will help the user interact with what you have to offer. Are you imparting practical information? Entertaining? Selling a product?
There are four core purposes common to all websites:
1.  **Describing Expertise** (e.g., a portfolio or blog)
2.  **Building Your Reputation** (e.g., company history, testimonials)
3.  **Generating Leads** (e.g., collecting emails, requesting quotes)
4.  **Sales and After Care** (e.g., e-commerce checkout, customer support)

### 2. Simplicity
Simplicity is the best way to improve user experience. 
*   **Color:** Color communicates messages and evokes emotional responses. Keep your color palette limited to **less than 5 colors**.
    *   *Modern Pro-Tip:* Use the **60-30-10 rule** (60% dominant color, 30% secondary, 10% accent color) and ensure high contrast for accessibility (WCAG standards).
*   **Type (Typography):** Typography commands attention and acts as the visual interpretation of the brand voice. Typefaces should be legible. Use a **maximum of 3 different fonts** on the entire website.
*   **Imagery:** Every visual aspect (photos, illustrations, video). High-quality images form an impression of professionalism and credibility. Most initial information consumed on a website is visual.

### 3. Navigation
Navigation is the wayfinding system of your website. If navigation is confusing, visitors will give up and go elsewhere. Keeping navigation simple, intuitive, and consistent on every page is key.

### 4. F-Shaped Pattern Reading
Eye-tracking studies have found that users scan websites in an "F" shape—reading across the top, then down the left side, then across again. 
*   An effectively designed website works *with* this natural pattern. Put the most critical information at the top and along the left-hand side.
*   *Modern Context:* This applies mostly to text-heavy pages. If your page is highly visual, users might scan in a "Z" pattern instead!

*Visual representation of how a user's eye tracks across a page.*

### 5. Visual Hierarchy
Visual hierarchy is the **arrangement of elements in order of importance**. It is established using size, color, imagery, contrast, typography, whitespace, texture, and style. One of the most important functions of visual hierarchy is to establish a **focal point**; this shows visitors where the most important information is.

### 6. Content
An effective website has both great design and great content. Using compelling language, great content can attract and influence visitors by converting them into customers.

### 7. Grid-Based Layout
Grids help to structure your design and keep your content organized. The grid helps align elements on the page and keep it clean. A grid-based layout arranges content into a clean, rigid structure with columns, sections that line up, and feels balanced. 
*   *Modern Context:* Modern web design heavily relies on CSS Grid and Flexbox to create these responsive, invisible grids.

### 8. Load Time
Waiting for a website to load will lose visitors. Nearly half of web visitors expect a site to load in 2 seconds or less, and they will potentially leave a site that is not loaded within 3 seconds. Optimizing image sizes (and code) will help load your site faster. 
*   *Modern Context:* Google measures this via **Core Web Vitals** (specifically LCP - Largest Contentful Paint). Aiming for under 2.5 seconds is the modern standard.

### 9. Mobile Friendly
More people are using their phones or other devices to browse the web than ever before. It is critical to build your website with a responsive layout where your website can adjust seamlessly to different screen sizes (mobile, tablet, desktop).

## C. Analogies and Use Cases

### The F-Shaped Reading Analogy
Think of the F-Shaped pattern like reading a newspaper. You don't read every single word of every article. You scan the massive headlines at the top (the top horizontal bar of the F), then scan down the left column of stories (the vertical spine of the F), stopping to read the first sentence of the ones that look interesting (the second horizontal bar of the F). 

### Use Case: The 3-Font Rule
If you use too many fonts (e.g., Comic Sans for the header, Arial for the body, Times New Roman for the sidebar, and Courier for the footer), the page looks chaotic and amateurish. Modern brands stick to a strict typography scale:
*   **Font 1:** A bold, unique font for main headers.
*   **Font 2:** A clean, readable font for body text.
*   **Font 3:** (Optional) A specialized font for buttons or callouts.

## D. Lesson Wrap-Up / FAQs

### Key Takeaways
*   Every website needs a clear **Purpose** (Sales, Leads, Expertise, or Reputation).
*   **Simplicity** dictates limiting colors (under 5) and fonts (max 3).
*   **Navigation** must be intuitive, and design should follow natural eye-tracking (**F-Pattern**).
*   Use **Visual Hierarchy** to show the user what is most important.
*   Speed and **Mobile-Friendliness** are non-negotiable in modern web design.

### Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Is the "less than 5 colors" rule strict?**
**A:** It's a guideline. Technically, you should have a primary brand color, a secondary color, an accent color, plus black/white for text and backgrounds. If you use 10 different vibrant colors, it will look messy.

**Q: What is a "responsive layout"?**
**A:** It means the website automatically re-arranges itself based on the screen size. A 3-column desktop layout might turn into a single vertical column on a mobile phone so users don't have to pinch and zoom to read.

**Q: What is a focal point?**
**A:** It is the very first thing you want a user to look at when they land on your page (usually a big headline or a prominent "Buy Now" button).
