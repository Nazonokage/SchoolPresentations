## B. Main Lesson

### Your Project Update Checklist
To complete this milestone, you must update your ongoing project document with the following five elements. Ensure everything is well-organized and clearly labeled.

1.  **Security Policies Chapter:**
    *   Create a dedicated chapter for the Security Policies you drafted in Module 10-11.
    *   Continuously add more policies as you brainstorm and align them with the client's needs.
2.  **User Access Control (UAC) Subsection:**
    *   Append a UAC subsection inside your Security Policies chapter.
    *   Create sample UAC levels (Level 1 to Level 4) that the organization can implement.
    *   Provide examples of how UAC is used within their specific operational context.
3.  **Authentication Subsection:**
    *   Append an Authentication subsection inside your Security Policies chapter.
    *   Provide specific recommendations for the organization (e.g., minimum 8 characters, requiring uppercase, numbers, and symbols).
    *   Recommend **Two-Factor Authentication (2FA)** for any systems they use, especially if they utilize social media platforms for business.
4.  **Applying RMF Step 2:**
    *   Within your Risk Management Framework table, add a new column titled "Selected Security Control."
    *   Fill in the specific security control you selected for each risk, along with its **Control Type** and **Control Function** (e.g., *Technical / Preventive: Firewall*).
    *   If more than one control applies to a single risk, add another row.
5.  **Presentation Preparation:**
    *   Begin creating a PowerPoint presentation for your Pre-Defense (P2 Exam). This will be presented to your teacher to ensure your project direction is correct.

## C. Analogies and Use Cases

### The "Consultant's Final Report" Analogy
Think of yourself as an independent security consultant who has been hired to audit a business. Throughout this course, you have done the groundwork: you identified the problems (risks), prioritized them (matrix), and designed the solutions (controls and policies).

This module is where you compile the **Client Deliverable**. You are taking all your raw notes, spreadsheets, and drafts and formatting them into a professional, easy-to-read report that your client can actually understand and implement. The PowerPoint is your "Executive Summary" presentation to the board of directors.

### Real-World Use Case: The Dental Clinic Audit
Imagine you are auditing a local dental clinic. 
*   **Risk:** Patient records are stored in an unlocked filing cabinet.
*   **Policy Recommendation:** "All physical patient records must be locked in a secure cabinet at the end of each shift."
*   **UAC Recommendation:** "Level 1 (Front Desk) can view appointment schedules but cannot edit medical history. Level 2 (Dentist) can edit medical history."
*   **RMF Step 2:** Select *Physical/Preventive* control: Install a heavy-duty lock on the filing cabinet.
*   **Presentation:** You show the clinic owner your risk matrix (which shows this is a "Most Important" priority) and walk them through the cost-benefit of implementing the lock.

## D. Lesson Wrap-Up / FAQs

### Key Takeaways
*   This module is a **synthesis milestone**; you are compiling all previous work into one cohesive document.
*   **Security Policies, UAC, and Authentication** are closely related and should be presented together in a "Security Policies" chapter.
*   The **RMF table** must now reflect not just the risk, but the selected **control type** and **function**.
*   The **Pre-Defense Presentation** requires you to present your findings, risk matrix, and recommendations clearly and concisely.

### Frequently Asked Questions
**1. I'm working solo. How do I present the "Project Members" section?**
**A:** Simply list yourself as the sole project member and author of the security report. You can also mention any hypothetical stakeholders or the client themselves if it helps frame the presentation.

**2. What if my selected security control doesn't fit perfectly into one type or function?**
**A:** Real-world security controls often overlap. Just choose the primary type and function that best describes it. For example, a security guard is *Physical* and can be *Preventive* (guarding the door) or *Detective* (patrolling). Just justify your choice briefly in your notes.

**3. Do I need to have the entire PowerPoint completely finished for this module?**
**A:** No, the goal is to *begin* creating it. You should aim to have the Introduction (Members, Organization Summary) and the Risk Management Framework (Risk Matrix) sections drafted at minimum.

**4. What is the most common mistake students make during the Pre-Defense?**
**A:** Getting too technical and forgetting the business context. Remember, your audience (the panel) may not know the deep technical details of SQL injection. Focus on *why* the risk matters to the business, *what* the control does, and *how* it protects their assets.

**5. What should I do with the "Concerns" section from the activity sheet?**
**A:** Treat this as a reflection log. Write down any challenges you faced (e.g., "Struggled to estimate likelihood scores" or "Couldn't find specific information about the organization's current security"). This shows self-awareness and critical thinking.

### Solo Project: Pre-Defense Milestone Submission
**Project Overview:** 
For Module 15, you will compile your entire project document into a final "Pre-Defense" package and create the initial slides for your presentation.

**Project Guidelines:**
*   **Format:** Individual work.
*   **Objective:** Submit a complete, updated project document and an initial presentation draft.
*   **Submission:** Organize your files (Word document, PDF, PowerPoint) in a dedicated folder.

**Step-by-Step Deliverable:**

**Part 1: The Project Document Update**
Ensure your document contains the following clearly labeled chapters:
1.  **Organization Overview:** Brief background of the chosen business.
2.  **Risk Management Framework:** Include your updated RMF table with Risk, Likelihood, Severity, Priority, and Selected Controls (Type & Function).
3.  **Security Policies:** Include the drafted policies, the UAC subsection (with levels), and the Authentication subsection (with password and 2FA recommendations).

*   **Slide 1:** Title Slide (Project Name, Your Name).

*   **Slide 3:** Risk Management Framework - Present your Risk Matrix visually.
*   **Slide 4:** Security Recommendations - Highlight your top 3 controls and policies.
*   **Slide 5:** Conclusion and Q&A slide.

> **Pro Tip:** Keep the presentation slides concise. Use bullet points rather than full paragraphs, and rely on your spoken presentation to provide the details.
