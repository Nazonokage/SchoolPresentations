## B. Main Lesson

### Project Completion Guidelines
Your objective is to finalize your project document using all the knowledge you have accumulated. Apply the following components to complete your Risk Management Framework (RMF) documentation:

1.  **RMF Step 3 (Implementation & Recommendations):**
    *   Create a chapter titled **"Recommendations."**
    *   For every risk identified in your earlier modules, you must provide a detailed recommendation. Use the knowledge from Module 18 to structure these.
2.  **RMF Step 4 (Assessment Framework):**
    *   Include a subsection in your Recommendations chapter titled **"Recommended Assessment."**
    *   Recommend to the organization the need for assessment for every recommendation/potential risk/security control provided. 
    *   State the **3 Assessment Questions** that must be answered by future assessors.
3.  **RMF Step 5 (Authorization):**
    *   Create a chapter titled **"Conclusion."**
    *   Write about the Authorized Information Systems step of the RMF. Remind the organization that they are free to apply your recommendations, but this action must be noted by their executives/owners.
    *   Inform them once more of the importance of having an IAS document for their organization.
4.  **RMF Step 6 (Monitoring):**
    *   Within your Conclusion chapter, add another paragraph explaining the purpose of having someone monitor the risks and security controls of the organization, regardless of whether they accepted your recommendations.
5.  **Additional Enrichment:**
    *   Ensure your document includes added risks and benefits (from the Fortuitous Events module).
    *   Ensure your recommendations include specific software, tools, and external services (Law Enforcement, Emergency Services, Legal Entities).

### Required Documentation Format
Adhere strictly to the following formatting guidelines for your final submission:

> **Formatting Specifications:**
> *   **Margins:** Narrow Margins
> *   **Line Spacing:** 1.5 spacing
> *   **Font:** Calibri, Size 11

### Required Document Structure (Chapters)
Your final documentation must be structured exactly as follows:

*   **I. Introduction**
    *   **A. About the Organization:** Describe the organization or business you are securing (employee numbers, operations, industry, etc.).
    *   **B. Why Choose This Organization:** Explain the significance of this organization to you (e.g., family-owned, friend's business, or a hypothetical case you developed).
    *   **C. About the Team Members:** Describe your role and responsibilities as the sole security consultant.
*   **II. Purpose of the Project:** Explain the purpose of the project beyond just being a course requirement (e.g., to improve their security posture, prevent data loss).
*   **III. Identified Risks**
    *   **A. Risk Management Framework:** Briefly describe the RMF and how you used it to identify risks.
    *   **B. Risk Matrix:** Describe and show the Risk Matrix you created that is suited for the organization you are protecting.
*   **IV. Recommendations**
    *   **A. Security Recommendations:** Provide your recommendations for every risk and security control identified.
    *   **B. Security Policies:** Provide essential security policies for the organization.
        *   **1. Recommended Authorization:** Recommend authorization policies (e.g., password rules, system access).
        *   **2. User Access Controls:** Recommend UAC for every system and document.
    *   **C. Recommended Assessment:** Recommend to the organization that there must be assessments, and instruct them to answer the 3 questions provided for assessors in the future.
*   **V. Conclusion**
    *   **A. Authorization:** Conclude that your project is essential for the organization and requires the authorization of the executives/owners.
    *   **B. Monitoring:** Conclude that regardless of whether they accept your recommendations, it is still recommended to have someone monitor risks and security in the organization.

### Finalizing the Presentation
Your Pre-Defense PowerPoint presentation must be finalized and practiced. Structure your slides as follows:

*   **I. Introduction**
    *   A. Members (Yourself)
    *   B. Summary of About the Organization
*   **II. Risk Management Framework**
    *   A. Risk Matrix
*   **III. Recommendations**
    *   A. Security Recommendations
    *   B. Security Policies
*   **IV. Q and A**

> **Goal of the Project Defense:** To present to the teacher/panelist that the project follows the standards of security, and to convince them that the organization will benefit from the project.

## D. Lesson Wrap-Up / FAQs

### Key Takeaways
*   This module is a **synthesis workshop**; no new concepts are introduced.
*   The final project document requires **5 major chapters** (Introduction, Purpose, Identified Risks, Recommendations, Conclusion).
*   You must strictly follow the formatting guidelines (Narrow margins, 1.5 spacing, Calibri 11).
*   The presentation must be concise and convincing, aiming to prove the standards of security are met.

### Frequently Asked Questions
**1. What if I am not ready to defend yet?**
**A:** The two-day window for Modules 24 and 25 is designed to give you ample time to finish. If you are ready, you can begin defending. If not, use the time to polish your document and practice your presentation.

**2. How do I structure the "Recommendations" chapter if I have many risks?**
**A:** Use subheadings or a table. For each risk, state the recommended control (Type and Function) and explain *why* it mitigates the risk. This keeps the document organized and scannable.

**3. Why do I need to mention "monitoring" if the organization might reject my recommendations?**
**A:** Because monitoring is a continuous process. Even if they accept the risk of a cyberattack, they still need to monitor their systems to know if and when that risk materializes. Monitoring is not optional for business continuity.

**4. What should I do if the panel asks a question I don't know the answer to?**
**A:** Be honest. State that you will look into it, or explain your reasoning based on the frameworks you have learned. A good security professional knows the limits of their knowledge and knows how to find the answer.

**5. How detailed should the "About the Organization" section be?**
**A:** Provide enough detail to give context to the risks. If it's a small retail store, mention the number of employees, what they sell, and how they handle customer data. This justifies the risks you identified.
