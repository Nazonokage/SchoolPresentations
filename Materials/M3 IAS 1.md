## B. Main Lesson

### Information Security in a Professional Perspective
Information security is not just the responsibility of the IT department. Every employee plays a role. In an organizational context, security officers must focus on protecting employee information, which includes:

*   Accounts and login details
*   Employee personal information
*   Salary information
*   User accounts
*   Computers and devices
*   Work schedules
*   Dissemination of information

> **Pro Tip:** Even if you are not a designated security officer, you must apply the security knowledge provided by your organization's security team to protect your accounts, passwords, and devices at home and at work.

### Legalities in Information Security
Laws are rules that mandate or prohibit certain societal behaviors. In short, they stop people from doing bad things. Here are the major laws you must follow in Information Security:

| Law / Regulation | Focus Area | Key Implications |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Copyright Law** | Protects original works of authorship (documents, code, media). | If your project is copied by another group without permission, it violates copyright law. |
| **Financial Reporting (Serbanes-Oxley Act)** | Requires accurate financial reporting from organizations. | Failure to report financial information properly can lead to heavy fines and even jail time. |
| **Digital Millennium Copyright Act (DMCA)** | A specialized copyright law focusing on digital media and software. | It protects the lines of code you write, the systems you build, and project documentation. |
| **Data Privacy Act** | Protects the privacy of personal data. | We will discuss this in detail later; it focuses on securing the privacy of your data. |

### Ethics in Information Security
Ethics define socially acceptable behavior. As an ethical technology user, you should follow the **Ten Commandments of Computer Ethics**:

1.  Thou shalt not use a computer to harm other people.
2.  Thou shalt not interfere with other people's computer work.
3.  Thou shalt not snoop around in other people's computer files.
4.  Thou shalt not use a computer to steal.
5.  Thou shalt not use a computer to bear false witness.
6.  Thou shalt not copy or use proprietary software for which you have not paid (without permission).
7.  Thou shalt not use other people's computer resources without authorization or proper compensation.
8.  Thou shalt not appropriate other people's intellectual output.
9.  Thou shalt think about the social consequences of the program you are writing or the system you are designing.
10. Thou shalt always use a computer in ways that ensure consideration and respect for other humans.

### Implications and Punishments
It is crucial to understand that **Laws carry sanctions, while Ethics do not.** 

Here is how violations are handled across the three domains:

| Domain | Type of Violation | Potential Consequences |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Professional** | Failing to follow organizational rules and policies. | Suspension, salary reduction, mandatory retraining, or termination (depending on severity). |
| **Legal** | Violating enacted laws (Copyright, DMCA, SOX, Data Privacy). | Fines, community service, and imprisonment (depending on the severity of the violated law). |
| **Ethical** | Violating social norms and ethical standards. | Damage to social status, loss of trust, avoidance by peers, severe reputation damage, and difficulty getting hired. |

### The CIA Triad
You may have already heard of these three core components of information security. Together, they form the **CIA Triad**:

![CIA Triad](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1563986768609-322da13575f3?auto=format&fit=crop&w=800&q=80)

*   **Confidentiality:** Ensuring that information is accessible only to those authorized to have access. 
    *   *Example:* Only you can access your university email or personal accounts.
*   **Integrity:** Safeguarding the accuracy and completeness of information and processing methods.
    *   *Example:* A memo about a canceled class includes the correct date, time, and the signature of the officer who declared it.
*   **Availability:** Ensuring that authorized users have access to information and associated assets when required.
    *   *Example:* Only students from the university can access the student portal, and they can recover their passwords if forgotten.

### Data Privacy Act
In the Philippines, we have **Republic Act No. 10173**, otherwise known as the **Data Privacy Act**. 

This is a law that seeks to protect all forms of information, whether private, personal, or sensitive. It covers both natural and juridical persons involved in the processing of personal information. 

> **Note:** This act focuses on the necessity of securing personal information. You may recall that whenever you fill out digital forms (like Google Forms), the first part usually asks for your consent to agree to the Data Privacy Act. This tells you that any information you write in that form is legally protected and secured.

## C. Analogies and Use Cases

### The Security Guard and the Rulebook Analogy
Think of an organization as a large building.
*   **Legal Issues** are the **Local Police and Government Laws**. If you steal from the building, you go to jail. 
*   **Professional Issues** are the **Building Security Guard and HR Policies**. If you break the building's rules, you get fired or suspended.
*   **Ethical Issues** are the **Social Contract among Tenants**. If you are rude, snoop on your neighbors, or act unprofessionally, people will avoid you, and you will lose your reputation in the community.

### Real-World Use Cases
1.  **Software Development & DMCA:** A developer copies a proprietary algorithm from a competitor's software without paying for it. This is a violation of the DMCA and Copyright Law, leading to massive lawsuits and financial damages for the company.
2.  **Data Privacy in Healthcare:** A hospital administrator accidentally emails a spreadsheet containing patient names and medical diagnoses to the wrong mailing list. This is a severe violation of the Data Privacy Act, resulting in heavy fines, mandatory breach notifications, and a massive loss of public trust.
3.  **Financial Reporting (SOX):** An IT manager is asked to alter database logs to hide a financial discrepancy. Complying with this request violates the Serbanes-Oxley Act (SOX), leading to personal criminal liability and jail time for the manager.

## D. Lesson Wrap-Up / FAQs

### Key Takeaways
*   **Legalities, Ethics, and Professionalism** are three distinct but interconnected pillars of Information Security.
*   **Laws** carry legal sanctions (fines, jail time), while **Ethics** carry social sanctions (loss of reputation, trust).
*   The **CIA Triad** (Confidentiality, Integrity, Availability) is the core framework for securing information.
*   The **Data Privacy Act** (RA 10173) mandates the protection of personal data and requires consent from users.
*   As an IT professional, you are responsible for knowing and enforcing these standards through training, awareness, and policy compliance.

### Frequently Asked Questions
**1. What is the difference between a legal issue and an ethical issue in IT?**
A legal issue involves breaking a specific, written law (e.g., stealing software), which results in legal punishment. An ethical issue involves breaking a moral code or social norm (e.g., snooping through a coworker's files without malicious intent), which results in social or professional consequences but not necessarily jail time.

**2. Why do I need to know about the Serbanes-Oxley Act (SOX) if I am not in finance?**
SOX requires strict controls over financial data and reporting. IT professionals often build, maintain, and secure the systems that store this financial data. If you alter or lose financial records due to negligence or malicious intent, you can be held legally liable under SOX.

**3. How does the Data Privacy Act affect everyday users?**
It gives users control over their personal information. Organizations must inform you about how your data is collected and used, and they must protect it from breaches. It also gives you the right to request the deletion of your data in certain circumstances.

**4. What happens if I violate ethical standards but not laws?**
You won't go to jail, but you could face severe professional and social consequences. You might be fired, blacklisted from future employment, or socially ostracized. In the tight-knit IT community, a bad reputation spreads quickly.

### Part 2: Check for Understanding (Essay)

**1. The CIA Triad stands for:**
*Answer:* Confidentiality, Integrity, and Availability.

**2. In developing systems, what part of the development process do we need to enforce copyright and DMCA, and why?**
*Guidance:* Copyright and DMCA enforcement should be implemented **during the entire Software Development Life Cycle (SDLC)**, but most crucially during the **Requirements Gathering** and **Design/Implementation** phases. 
*   **Why?** During requirements gathering, you must ensure the software requirements do not infringe on existing patents or copyrights. During implementation, developers must ensure they are not copying proprietary code without a license. Furthermore, enforcing compliance early prevents costly legal battles, fines, and the need to rewrite entire sections of code later in the project.

**3. Compare and contrast the consequences of violating a law versus violating an ethical standard in the IT profession.**
*Guidance:* Violating a law (e.g., DMCA, SOX) carries formal sanctions from the government, such as fines, imprisonment, or community service. Violating an ethical standard (e.g., snooping on a colleague's files, failing to respect user privacy even when technically legal) carries social and professional sanctions. These include termination from a job, loss of professional reputation, difficulty finding future employment, and social ostracization. While laws are enforced by the state, ethics are enforced by the community and one's own moral compass.
