## B. Main Lesson

### Comprehensive Study Guide

To help you prepare, here is a summary of the key concepts you need to review for this quiz:

**1. Risk Management Fundamentals**
*   **Risk vs. Threat:** A risk is the potential for loss or damage. A threat is the actor or event that exploits a vulnerability to cause that risk.
*   **Risk Identification:** Understanding your **Assets** (what you own), **Vulnerabilities** (weaknesses), **Threats** (attackers or events), and **Controls** (existing protections).
*   **Risk Assessment:** Measuring the **Likelihood** (probability) and **Impact** (severity/cost) of a risk.
*   **Risk Treatment Options:** Remediation (remove), Reduction (mitigate), Transfer (insurance/third party), Accept (acknowledge), or Avoid (eliminate the activity).
*   **The 5-Step Risk Management Process:** Identification, Assessing, Treatment, Communication, Re-identification.

**2. Security Architecture Design**
*   **Goal:** Create a comprehensive framework to ensure business continuity and protect Confidentiality, Integrity, and Availability (CIA Triad).
*   **Key Components:** Threat Assessment, Risk Management, Access Control, Network Security, Data Protection, Incident Response, Awareness Training, and Continuous Evaluation.
*   **Software Development:** Integrating security into the SDLC (Secure Development Practices, AuthN/AuthZ, Secure Communication, Input Validation, etc.).

**3. Business Continuity & IT Disaster Recovery**
*   **Business Continuity:** The big-picture strategy to keep the entire business running during a disaster.
*   **IT Disaster Recovery Plan (IT DRP):** Focuses specifically on restoring technology (servers, networks, data) after a disruption.
*   **3 Steps of IT DRP (Maintenance Phase):** 1. Identify system activities. 2. Identify hindrances. 3. Determine max tolerable downtime.

Think of taking this quiz like responding to a security incident:

In professional certification exams (like CompTIA Security+ or CISSP), the questions are often scenario-based. For the essay portion of this quiz, focus on *why* something is important (e.g., "Why is continuous monitoring important?"). Employers want to hire security professionals who understand the *reasoning* behind the frameworks, not just those who memorize the steps.

## D. Lesson Wrap-Up / FAQs

### Key Takeaways for the Quiz
*   Risk cannot be completely eliminated; it can only be managed.
*   The Risk Matrix maps Likelihood against Impact to determine priority.
*   Incident Response Plans are vital for Business Continuity.
*   IT DRP focuses on recovery, while Business Continuity focuses on keeping the lights on.
*   The 5th step of Risk Management (Re-identification) is crucial because the threat landscape is constantly evolving.

## F. Skill-Building Check

### First Period Quiz (Actual Assessment)

*This is the actual quiz. Write your answers in a separate document or as instructed by your teacher.*

#### Part 1: Multiple Choice (10 Pts, 2pts each)
*Encircle or write the letter of the best answer from the given choices.*

**1. From your understanding so far, is it possible to completely remove a risk?**
a. A firm yes
b. Yes, but it needs to remove the asset at risk
c. No, not possible
d. Yes, but very rarely
**Correct Answer: C** (It is practically impossible to remove all risks entirely. You can reduce them, transfer them, or avoid them, but in the real world, residual risk always exists).

**2. Which of the following is not a method in identifying risks?**
a. Through Vulnerabilities
b. Through Controls
c. Through Assets
d. None of the above
**Correct Answer: D** (All of these are used to identify risks. Looking at assets, vulnerabilities, threats, and existing controls provides a complete picture).

**3. Which of the following is an important consideration in security architecture design for business continuity?**
a. Confidentiality of data during normal operations
b. Protection against denial-of-service attacks
c. Development of a structured incident response plan
d. Compliance with industry regulations
**Correct Answer: C** (An incident response plan is specifically designed to ensure business continuity during a crisis).

**4. Which is the most important part of disaster recovery?**
a. Finding what caused the problem
b. The solution or temporary solution
c. The costs
d. All of the above
**Correct Answer: B** (The core goal of Disaster Recovery is to restore operations. The solution or temporary solution gets the business back online).

**5. Which of this is not performed after disaster recovery?**
a. Investigation
b. Reporting
c. Training
**Correct Answer: C** (Training is a preventive measure performed *before* a disaster to prepare staff. Investigation and reporting are post-incident activities).

#### Part 2: Essay (20 Pts)
*Answer the following questions in your own words.*

**1. What are the 2 factors determining the overall risk, and how do you measure them? (5pts)**
*Guidance:* The 2 factors are **Likelihood** (probability of occurrence) and **Impact** (severity or cost). You measure them using a scale (e.g., 1 to 5) on a Risk Matrix to calculate the overall priority level of the risk.

**2. Describe the 4 steps of disaster recovery plan. (10pts)**
*Guidance:* Based on standard IT DRP principles:
1. **Risk Assessment / Asset Identification:** Identify the critical activities and assets of the system.
2. **Business Impact Analysis (BIA):** Identify what issues could hinder the activity and determine how long the business can survive without it.
3. **Recovery Strategy Development:** Select and implement the controls (e.g., backup servers, offsite data storage) to handle the identified issues.
4. **Testing and Maintenance:** Regularly test the plan to ensure it works and update it as the system evolves.

**3. Describe the 5th step of risk management and explain why it is important. (5pts)**
*Guidance:* The 5th step is **Re-identification (or Rinse-and-Repeat)**. It is important because the risk environment is dynamic. A control that was effective today might become obsolete tomorrow, and new threats constantly emerge. Continuously re-evaluating risks ensures the organization does not miss new details or vulnerabilities.
