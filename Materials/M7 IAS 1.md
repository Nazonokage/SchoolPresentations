## B. Main Lesson

### The Project Update Checklist
For this update, you will expand your current project document by applying the following five elements. 

1.  **Legal, Ethical, and Professional Issues:** Identify risks that could arise from these issues within your chosen organization. For example, what happens if they mishandle customer data (Data Privacy) or copy software without a license (Copyright)?
2.  **Risk Identification and Assessment:** 
    *   Brainstorm every risk you can think of. If you can, interview the business owner or research common risks for that industry.
    *   Determine the **Likelihood** of each risk (scale of 1-5).
    *   Determine the **Severity** of each risk (scale of 1-5).
3.  **Risk Management Framework (RMF) Table:** Finalize the layout for your RMF table. Create a structured document that lists all your identified risks.
4.  **Risk Matrix:** Create a custom risk matrix that fits the specific **Risk Appetite** of your chosen organization. 
5.  **Applying Step 1 of RMF:** Using your custom risk matrix, determine the priority level of each risk (e.g., Least Important, Most Important) and add this as a column in your RMF table.

### Understanding the Deliverables
At the end of this module, you will have a single, comprehensive document (or set of documents) that outlines the initial risk assessment for your chosen target. This document will be the foundation for the next phases of the course, where you will begin selecting and implementing security controls.

## C. Analogies and Use Cases

### The "Architect's Blueprint" Analogy
Think of yourself as an architect. In the previous modules, you learned about materials (legal/ethics), structural stresses (risk identification), and building codes (RMF). 
In this module, you are drawing the first draft of the blueprint. You are not building the house yet (which is the next step in the RMF), but you are assembling all the measurements and priorities onto one page so that the construction team knows exactly where to start.

### Real-World Use Case: The Local Bookstore (Solo Project)
Imagine you are a solo consultant hired by a local independent bookstore.
*   **Legal/Ethical Risk:** They store customer names and email addresses on a spreadsheet. This violates the Data Privacy Act if not properly secured.
*   **Risk Identification:** Flooding (physical), Point-of-Sale system hacking (digital), employee theft (internal).
*   **Risk Matrix:** The bookstore owner has a moderate risk appetite. They can tolerate a little downtime, but losing customer data is catastrophic. Your matrix will reflect that "Most Important" risks are those affecting data integrity.
*   **Output:** You create a table that lists these risks, their likelihood and impact, and their calculated priority.

## D. Lesson Wrap-Up / FAQs

### Key Takeaways
*   This module is a **synthesis activity**; you are combining the knowledge from Modules 2 through 6 into a single project update.
*   Legal and ethical issues are critical inputs into your risk identification process.
*   The Risk Matrix is not one-size-fits-all; it must reflect the organization's specific **Risk Appetite**.
*   **Documentation is key.** You will be assessed on your ability to clearly communicate these risks.

### Frequently Asked Questions
**1. Since I am working solo, how do I get information about the business?**
**A:** If you are unable to interview a real business owner, select a hypothetical or publicly known small business (like a local cafe or an online freelancer). Research common risks for that industry and simulate their risk appetite based on their size and sector.

**2. How many risks should I identify?**
**A:** Aim for a minimum of 5–8 distinct risks. This gives you enough variety to properly demonstrate the Likelihood, Severity, and Prioritization aspects of the matrix.

**3. What are "Notable Changes" and "Concerns" in the project documentation?**
**A:** These are reflection questions. "Notable Changes" are updates or pivots you had to make during your research (e.g., "I realized the business relies heavily on cloud storage, so I added Cloud Vulnerabilities to the risk list"). "Concerns" are challenges you face (e.g., "The business owner was reluctant to share their network setup" or "I am struggling to estimate the financial impact of a breach").

**4. Do I need to create a Google Drive folder?**
**A:** For the purposes of this course, you should maintain a well-organized digital portfolio (a dedicated folder on your computer or cloud storage). Include all your files from Modules 2 to 7 in this folder so you can easily track your progress and submit them when required.

## E. Guided Drills / Code Projects

### Solo Project: Complete Risk Assessment & Matrix
**Project Overview:** 
For Module 7, you will produce a finalized draft of your **Risk Assessment Table** and **Custom Risk Matrix**. This is your first major milestone.

**Project Guidelines:**
*   **Format:** Individual work.
*   **Objective:** Apply the 5-step checklist from the Main Lesson to your chosen organization.

**Step-by-Step Deliverable:**

**Step 1: Choose Your Organization**
Select a small business, a fictional company, or a university department. *Example: "The Cozy Nook Bookstore."*

**Step 2: Identify Legal/Ethical Risks**
Write down 2 legal or ethical risks specific to this business (e.g., "Mishandling customer credit card data violates PCI DSS" or "Using unlicensed images on their website violates DMCA").

**Step 3: Complete the Risk Identification Table**
Brainstorm 5-8 total risks. Assign them a Likelihood (1-5) and Severity (1-5) score.

| Risk Identified | Likelihood (1-5) | Severity (1-5) | Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| *Example: POS System Hack* | *3* | *5* | *Customer financial data at risk.* |
| *Example: Server Room Flooding* | *2* | *4* | *Local weather patterns.* |
| *Example: Employee Data Leak* | *3* | *4* | *Lack of internal security training.* |
| *Example: Website Defacement* | *4* | *2* | *Reputational damage, low financial impact.* |

**Step 4: Create Your Custom Risk Matrix**
Draw a 5x5 grid. Based on the bookstore's **Risk Appetite**, shade the "Most Important" (highest priority) boxes differently from the "Least Important" boxes. 

> *Tip:* For a bookstore, perhaps any risk with a Severity of 5 is automatically "Most Important," regardless of Likelihood.

**Step 5: Categorize (RMF Step 1)**
Update your table from Step 3 to include a final column: `Priority Level`. Based on your custom matrix, assign the priority.

| Risk Identified | Likelihood | Severity | Priority Level (from Matrix) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| *POS System Hack* | *3* | *5* | *Most Important* |
| *Server Room Flooding* | *2* | *4* | *Important* |
| *Employee Data Leak* | *3* | *4* | *More Important* |
| *Website Defacement* | *4* | *2* | *Least Important* |

**Step 6: Write Your Reflection**
At the bottom of your document, write a short paragraph addressing:
*   **Notable Changes:** What surprising risks did you discover during your research?
*   **Concerns:** What was the most difficult part of this solo project?

## F. Skill-Building Check

### Part 1: Short Answer

**1. Why is it important to consider Legal and Ethical issues when identifying risks?**
*Guidance:* Legal issues carry sanctions like fines and jail time (e.g., Data Privacy Act). Ethical issues carry social and professional sanctions. Identifying them as risks ensures the organization doesn't just secure their technical systems, but also remains compliant and maintains public trust.

**2. In your own words, what is the purpose of the First Project Update?**
*Guidance:* The purpose is to synthesize the foundational concepts (risk identification, legal/ethical considerations, RMF, and Risk Matrix) into a single, coherent project document that serves as the baseline for future security planning.

A. The size of the company's IT budget.
B. The organization's Risk Appetite.
C. The number of employees in the company.
D. The complexity of the software they use.
**Correct Answer: B** (Risk Appetite is the core factor determining how risk is prioritized).
