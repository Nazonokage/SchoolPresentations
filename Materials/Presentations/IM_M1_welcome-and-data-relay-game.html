Introduction · 1

Why manage information?

Every day we create and use data: messages, photos, grades, receipts. Information management is about handling that data well so it can become information people use to decide things.

Organize

Give data a clear structure so nothing gets lost.

Store

Keep it safe, in a place it can be found again.

Retrieve

Get the right piece quickly when someone needs it.

Distribute

Share the result with the people who need it.

Introduction · 2

Data vs. information

Data

Raw, unorganized facts and figures, like a list of numbers.

Example: 18, 7, 11, 4

Information

Processed, organized output that has meaning and supports decisions, like a sales report showing trends.

Example: “Rice meals sold most today.”

An information system (IS) turns the first into the second: it collects, stores, processes and distributes data.

Analogy

The school canteen

  • Data: each order slip (item, quantity, break time).
  • Process: tally the slips per item.
  • Information: “Rice meals sold most.”
  • Decision: the cook prepares more rice meals tomorrow.

Same slips, two outcomes: a messy pile helps nobody; an organized tally helps the canteen make a decision. Good information management is the difference.

Game · Data Relay

Be the information system

Goal

Turn a pile of raw data slips into a short report that answers a decision question, faster and more accurately than the other teams.

Decision question

“Which two items should the canteen prepare more of tomorrow?”

This is one data slip. Each team gets about 30, and the teacher prepares them.

Teams of 4. Each team also gets folders, pens and a rule card.

Game · Data Relay

Meet your team

Keeper

Hardware: sorts and stores slips in labeled folders.

Processor

Software: tallies the slips.

Checker

Procedures: checks each step against the rule card.

Presenter

People: reports the information and the recommendation.

Game · Data Relay

The relay in motion

Watch the slips (the data) travel from the pile, through the team, to the report.

The pile is the data. The report the Presenter gives is the information.

Game · Data Relay

How a round works

The slips themselves are the data. Each team passes them through four stages.

  1. Collect: the team gathers and reads the slips.
  2. Store: the Keeper sorts them into labeled folders.
  3. Process: the Processor tallies per item; the Checker confirms.
  4. Distribute: the Presenter states the answer to the class.

Worked example · 3 Process

Tally and find the average

The Processor counts the orders, adds the quantities, then finds the average quantity per order.

ItemOrdersTotal qtyAverage per order
Rice meal599 ÷ 5 = 1.8
Noodles455 ÷ 4 = 1.25
Sandwich366 ÷ 3 = 2

Average = total quantity ÷ number of orders. Example: Rice meal sold 9 across 5 orders, so 9 ÷ 5 = 1.8.

Worked example · 4 Check

Check before you share

  1. Count the slips: 5 + 4 + 3 = 12. The raw table has 12 slips. They match.
  2. Add the quantities: 9 + 5 + 6 = 20. The Qty column of the raw table also adds up to 20.
  3. Recount one folder with a second person.
  4. If the totals differ, look for a slip in the wrong folder or one counted twice.

The Checker uses the rule card for this step. Procedures keep the result accurate.

Worked example · 5 Distribute

Share the information

Recommendation

Prepare more Rice meal (9 sold) and Sandwich (6 sold) tomorrow.

Notice: Sandwich has the highest average per order (2), but total quantity decides the recommendation.

Game · Data Relay

Surprise cards

Breach card

The teacher passes by. Any slip left unguarded is taken (−1).

Privacy card

A slip includes a student’s name. Hide it before presenting (+1).

System down

The Keeper must stop for 30 seconds. Work without the stored data.

Each card mirrors a real issue: data security, data privacy and dependence on technology.
