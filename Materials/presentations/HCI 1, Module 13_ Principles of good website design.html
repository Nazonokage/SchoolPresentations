Form and functionality

An effective website fulfills its intended function. Consistency, colors, typography, imagery, simplicity and functionality all contribute.

In a nutshell

A well-designed website builds trust and guides visitors to take action. Optimize for both form (aesthetics) and functionality (ease of use).

Principle 1

Website purpose

Your site must meet the user’s needs. A simple, clear intention on every page helps users interact with what you offer. Four core purposes are common to all websites.

Describing expertise

A portfolio or blog.

Building your reputation

Company history and testimonials.

Generating leads

Collecting emails, requesting quotes.

Sales and after care

E-commerce checkout and customer support.

Principle 2

Simplicity

Color

Communicates messages and evokes emotion. Keep the palette under 5 colors. A guideline: a primary, a secondary, an accent, plus black and white. Modern tip: the 60-30-10 rule and high contrast (WCAG).

Typography

Commands attention and interprets the brand voice. Legible typefaces, a maximum of 3 fonts.

Imagery

Photos, illustrations, video. High quality signals professionalism and credibility.

Principles 3 and 4

Navigation and the F-shaped pattern

Navigation

The wayfinding system. Confusing navigation sends visitors elsewhere. Keep it simple, intuitive and consistent on every page.

F-shaped reading

Eye tracking shows users read across the top, down the left, then across again. Put critical information at the top and along the left.

Modern context

Mostly for text-heavy pages. Highly visual pages may be scanned in a Z pattern.

Principle 5

Visual hierarchy

The arrangement of elements in order of importance.

Tools

Size, color, imagery, contrast, typography, whitespace, texture and style.

Focal point

One of its most important jobs: showing visitors where the most important information is.

Principles 6 and 7

Content and grid-based layout

Content

Great design needs great content. Compelling language attracts visitors and converts them into customers.

Grid-based layout

Columns and sections that line up give a clean, rigid, balanced structure.

Modern context

CSS Grid and Flexbox create responsive, invisible grids.

Principles 8 and 9

Load time and mobile friendly

2 s

Nearly half of visitors expect a load in 2 seconds or less.

3 s

Visitors may leave a site not loaded within 3 seconds.

2.5 s

Modern standard: Core Web Vitals LCP under 2.5 seconds.

Real-life scenarios

A newspaper and a font pile-up

The newspaper

You do not read every word. You scan the big headlines (top bar of the F), then the left column (the spine), reading the first sentence of the interesting ones (second bar).

Too many fonts

Header in Comic Sans

Body in Arial

Sidebar in Times

Footer in Courier

Chaotic and amateurish.

The 3-font rule

Font 1: bold, unique, for headers. Font 2: clean, for body. Font 3: optional, for buttons or callouts.

Recap

Five things to take with you

Every website needs a clear purpose: sales, leads, expertise or reputation.
Simplicity: fewer than 5 colors, at most 3 fonts.
Navigation must be intuitive, following the F-pattern.
Use visual hierarchy to show what matters most.
Speed and mobile friendliness are non-negotiable.
