The 8 golden rules, part 1

Rules 1 to 4

1. Be consistent

Similar patterns, identical terminology, homogeneous menus. A green Submit should not turn blue on the next page.

2. Enable shortcuts

Experts need speed: Ctrl + C, Ctrl + V and quick-access features.

3. Give informative feedback

Do not just say “invalid.” Show what is required and how strong the input is.

4. Yield closure

Sequences need a beginning, middle and end. A confirmation page gives satisfaction and relief.

The 8 golden rules, part 2

Rules 5 to 8

5. Prevent errors

Grey out unavailable items. Block letters in a phone field. If an error happens, explain it and offer a fix.

6. Reverse actions easily

Undo, Redo and Back relieve anxiety and encourage exploring.

7. Support internal locus of control

Users want to feel in charge, like “Exit without saving?” in Microsoft Office.

8. Minimize memory load

Let the computer remember. Use autofill. Choose recognition over recall.

Six key principles

The backbone of great UI

Clarity

Intuitive navigation, highly visible buttons. Is it clear what this does?

Familiarity

Menus at the top, and Back goes to the previous step, not the homepage.

Hierarchy

Show importance with color, size and fonts to guide the eye.

Negative space

Room to breathe makes important elements stand out.

Flexibility

Accelerators for power users, learnable for novices.

User control

Clearly marked emergency exits, plus undo and redo.

Essential GUI guidelines

Technical habits for graphical interfaces

Predictability

Common elements behave as expected, like pinch-to-zoom.

Discoverability

Label icons. Use affordances like button shadows.

Simplicity

Include only what serves the user’s purpose.

Alignment and readability

Edge alignment usually beats center. Mind contrast and type.

Efficiency

Fewer actions. One chief function per page. Progressive disclosure.

Proximity

Put controls near the objects they control.

Avoid dark patterns

Honesty keeps trust

Manipulative

Pre-filled, tiny and easy to miss. Hidden unsubscribe buttons work the same way.

Honest

Off by default. The user chooses.

Dark patterns trick users into things they did not intend, like surprise subscriptions or items sneaked into a cart.

Real-life scenarios

Rules in the wild

Undo is a safety net

With a net you walk fast and confident. Without one you freeze.

Memory load in a store

Retyping the same address for shipping, billing and the card is exhausting. One checkbox, “Billing address is the same as shipping,” fixes it.

Progressive disclosure

App settings show Notifications and Profile first. Advanced privacy options appear only when you open that category.

Recap

Five things to take with you

Consistency and familiarity make users feel at home.
Feedback and closure keep users informed and satisfied.
Preventing errors and easy undo create a safe place to explore.
Hierarchy and negative space guide the eye.
Flexibility serves both novices and experts.
