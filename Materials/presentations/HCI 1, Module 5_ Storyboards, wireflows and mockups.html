Why UX tools matter

Explaining a complex app idea with words alone is hard. UX tools let teams confirm the right features early, which saves cost, cuts development risk and brings in real end-user feedback.

Pro tip

UI/UX design is iterative. You are making a plan developers will build, so the more detailed the plan, the smoother the development.

Development stages

From idea to final product

1. Ideation and confirmation

Wireframing is visual, fast, cheap and easy to change. It confirms ideas with customers and avoids costly rework.

2. Screen flow design

Storyboards and wireflows. A storyboard is a single, linear narrative. A wireflow combines wireframe layouts with a simplified flowchart.

3. High-fidelity design

A GUI builder with drag-and-drop editors. A prototype is a mid-to-high fidelity representation that simulates interaction.

Storyboards

A visual depiction of scenes, dialog and action in order

It mocks up website ideas by presenting a sequence of wireframes in a specific order.

Plan in order

Helps UX developers plan features sequentially.

Cheap feedback

A lightweight way to get early feedback before heavy development.

Engaging

Shows how users will interact with the system.

Traditionally done in PowerPoint. Automated tools now simplify it.

How to storyboard a website

Seven steps

1. Collect the information

All text, images and content for the site.

2. Sort into topics

Start broad, work toward the narrowest details.

3. Determine the structure

Page titles, headings, subheadings and content.

4. Plan how pages link

Linear, hierarchical or branch.

5. Lay out the menu

One logical menu across all pages avoids redoing it.

6. Lay out the template page

A consistent interior layout.

7. Test the layout

Enter on any page. Can you navigate easily? If not, start again.

Storyboard step 4

Three ways pages can link

Linear

One page after another, like a story.

Hierarchical

A parent page leads to its children.

Branch

A web where pages connect in several directions.

Wireflows

Wireframes plus a flowchart

A wireflow shows how pages connect and interact. For an online flower shop:

Product page→Cart→Shipping info→Payment→Confirmation

Wireflow

The structure and the connections between pages.

Storyboard

The narrative: exactly what the user sees as they move through those steps.

Mockups

A full-size model of the final design

Mid-fidelity

Placeholder images and text.

High-fidelity

A complete visual layout with real colors, typography and content.

Upgrade a wireframe

Add color, typography and other visual features. Tools: Figma, InVision, Sketch.

Creating a mockup

Four things to keep in mind

Experiment

The best time to try colors, fonts, images and UI elements.

Style the UI

Navigation bars, buttons, links, sliders, forms and content containers.

Mobile first

Only necessary elements on mobile, then scale to tablet and desktop.

Avoid dummy text

Skip Lorem Ipsum. Real content prevents false layouts and unsatisfying results.

Best practice

Use digital tools for high-fidelity mockups so you can export a PSD or an SVG to show clients and hand off to developers.

Real-life scenarios

A movie set and a flower shop

Sketches

The director’s scribbled notes on a napkin.

Wireframes

The blueprint of the set.

Storyboards

The comic-strip version of the script, scene by scene.

Mockups

The actual set, with lighting and props, ready to film.

Online flower shop

Wireflow: Product page, Cart, Shipping info, Payment, Confirmation.

Storyboard: the sequence of what the user sees through those steps.

Mockup: pastel pinks and greens, high-quality bouquet photos and real text on the “Order Now” buttons.

Recap

Five things to take with you

Storyboards focus on narrative and sequence across pages.
Wireflows combine wireframes with flowcharts.
Mockups are full-size, visually complete, mid to high fidelity.
Good mockups use real content and are mobile first.
Test your flow from a user’s perspective before development.
Activity: identification

Sketch, wireframe, storyboard or mockup?

1. Design emerged during the medieval period.
2. A structural model of the design.
3. A full-size model of a design that demonstrates features of the software.
4. A tool that supports the process of making, not the actual design itself.
5. An easy way to see what information goes on the design in sequential order.
6. Can be turned into a prototype to provide dynamic functionalities.
7. Its purpose is to communicate and explore the concepts that come out of sketching.
8. Rapid, freehand drawing.
9. An outline of a design.
10. Lets you see how the site navigation should be set up so you avoid redoing menus or content on multiple pages.
