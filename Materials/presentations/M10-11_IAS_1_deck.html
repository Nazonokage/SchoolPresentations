Security policy

Simple rules are policies

Definition
A document containing rules and regulations within an organization. It protects the organization’s physical and IT assets, its employees and its customers.
Two everyday examples
Log out after using the work computer: no traces remain, and no one else can access your data. Wear your ID: guards and staff can tell who belongs; people without IDs are treated as suspicious.

Pro tip: policies look ordinary and easy to follow. That is their beauty: simple, actionable and effective.

In-depth policies

What a formal policy document holds

Process
Created by security officers, read by every employee and approved by executives, like the Authorization step in the RMF.
Contents
  • Objectives/purpose
  • Scope
  • The policy itself
  • Technical guidelines
  • Reporting guidelines/requirements

In this course, write simple, straightforward policies anyone can follow.

Policies by industry

Policies vary with the business

General / offices
No loitering. No personal accounts on work computers. Clean, clear desk. No food or drinks. Handle documents carefully. Always make backups. Keep documents secure and important drawers locked. No unauthorized visitors. No smoking.
Restaurants
Clean, clear kitchen table. Always clean the bathroom. Always someone at the cash register and someone to greet customers. Accessible fire extinguisher. No smoking. Always wear a hairnet or chef’s hat.
Factory / laboratory
Wear safety equipment. Accessible fire extinguisher. Inform others or executives when using equipment. Turn off computers and air conditioners after use. Clear workstation. Be accompanied by one or more persons.
Developer / coder
Secure your accounts. Write clean code. Document code clearly. Never delete, only archive. Always make backups.
User access control

Limiting what users can do

UAC is the practice of limiting how users can change, delete, update, create or view things in a document or any part of the system. It reduces or prevents damage and unauthorized alterations.

LevelTitlePermissions
4Creator / admin (highest)Create, input, edit, comment, view and modify the whole system; add other users to levels.
3High employeeDepends on rights given: usually input, edit, comment, view, sometimes add users.
2EmployeeEdit, comment and view.
1Customer (lowest)View, and sometimes comment.

Levels differ by organization: a small business may need 3, a large enterprise 5 or more.

UAC example

Fast food menu pricing display

Level 4 · Owners
Create menus to display and provide basic pricing.
Level 3 · Managers
Modify the displayed menu and the pricing.
Level 2 · Employees or staff
Modify the displayed menu depending on item availability.
Level 1 · Customers
View the menu and its pricing.

Analogy and use case

Hotel keycards and the grades portal

Hotel keycards
L4 general manager: master key to every room, maintenance and accounting. L3 housekeeping supervisor: guest rooms and supply closet, not accounting. L2 housekeeper: guest rooms to clean. L1 guest: own room and the lobby.
University grades portal
L4 system admin: new semesters, enroll students, database structure. L3 registrar or dean: view all grades, approve changes, reports. L2 teacher: input and edit grades only for assigned subjects. L1 student: view only their own grades.

Questions answered

Frequently asked

Are all policies simple?
No. Some, such as software development standards or encryption protocols, need specialized knowledge. The most effective policies for general staff are often the simplest.
Why have UAC?
To prevent accidental or malicious damage by limiting who can edit, delete or view sensitive information.
Which level to assign?
Follow the principle of least privilege: the lowest level needed for the job.
Do policies change?
Yes. Review and update them for new threats, technology and organizational changes.
Key quotes

Definitions worth remembering

Security policy
A document containing rules and regulations within an organization.
User access control
Limiting how users can change, delete, update, create or view parts of a system.
Least privilege
Assign the lowest level of access necessary to do the job.
Approval
Executives approve formal security policies.
Recap

Key takeaways

  • Security policies are formal rules that protect assets, approved by executives.
  • Policies vary by industry but aim at a safe, secure environment.
  • UAC restricts users by role, from level 1 (view) to level 4 (admin).
  • UAC applies to every aspect of the organization’s system and documentation.
