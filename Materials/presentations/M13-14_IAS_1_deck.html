Control types

What the control is made of

Security controls are safeguards or countermeasures prescribed for an information system to protect the confidentiality, integrity and availability of the system and its information.

Physical
Physical limitations that prevent a criminal or unauthorized person from accessing assets. Tangible: you can touch or hold them. Doors, locks, gates, CCTV, repairing broken infrastructure.
Technical
Intangible tools, mainly software on an organization’s computers and networks. Firewalls, IDS, patching a system, robust password checking.
Administrative
Policies, procedures and guidelines that enforce the organization’s attitude and ethics; often documentation. Security policies, access rights, risk management plans, password-making policies.
Control functions

What the control does

Preventive
Designed to stop unwanted or unauthorized activity before it starts.
Detective
Implemented to detect and alert to unwanted or unauthorized activity in progress or after it has occurred.
Corrective
Measures to repair damage or restore resources and capabilities to their prior state after unwanted activity: fix what was broken and make it function better.

Analogy

The fortress

Physical
  • Preventive: high stone walls and the moat.
  • Detective: guards patrolling with torches.
  • Corrective: masons repairing the wall after a catapult hit.
Other types
  • Administrative, preventive: the King’s law: anyone caught outside after dark will be arrested.
  • Technical, detective: a magical alarm spell that rings when someone touches the gate (IDS).

Questions answered

Frequently asked

Cover every type and function?
Rarely every combination. The RMF is a cycle: keep adding controls as you find new risks.
Type vs function?
Type: what the control is made of (lock, software, policy). Function: what it does (prevents, detects, corrects).
One control, several functions?
Yes. A security guard can prevent (at the door), detect (patrolling) and correct (escorting an intruder out). Specialized controls per function are often better.
Why administrative controls?
Technology cannot fix human error. A lock is useless if an employee holds the door open for a stranger.
Key quotes

Definitions worth remembering

Control types
Describe the nature of the control: physical (tangible), technical (software or hardware), administrative (policies).
Control functions
Describe the purpose: preventive (stops), detective (finds), corrective (fixes).
Strategy
The most effective strategies combine multiple types and functions.
Administrative controls
Just as important as physical and technical controls.
Recap

Key takeaways

  • Type is the nature of the control; function is its purpose.
  • Combine several types and several functions.
  • Administrative controls matter as much as physical and technical ones.
  • The RMF is a cycle: keep adding controls as risks change.
