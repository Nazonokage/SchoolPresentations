This module
No new theory. Finalize your Security Policies, user access controls and authentication recommendations, update your RMF table, and begin the presentation for your Pre-Defense (P2 Exam). Solo project: you are the independent security consultant.
Quick review
  • Security policies: rules that protect physical and IT assets.
  • UAC: limiting access by role (level 1 to 4).
  • Authentication: proving identity (passwords, 2FA).
  • RMF step 2: select controls (types and functions).

Analogy and use case

Final report and dental clinic

Consultant’s final report
You identified problems (risks), prioritized them (matrix) and designed solutions (controls and policies). Now you compile the client deliverable. The PowerPoint is the executive summary for the board.
Dental clinic audit
Risk: records in an unlocked cabinet. Policy: lock records at the end of each shift. UAC: Level 1 (front desk) views schedules, cannot edit medical history; Level 2 (dentist) can. RMF step 2: physical / preventive: a heavy-duty lock. Presentation: show the matrix (“Most Important”) and the cost-benefit of the lock.

Course project

Part 2: the presentation draft

Mandatory slides
  1. Title (project name, your name)
  2. Introduction (organization summary)
  3. RMF: present your risk matrix visually
  4. Security recommendations: your top 3 controls and policies
  5. Conclusion and Q and A
Pro tip
Keep slides concise. Use bullet points, not full paragraphs, and give the details by speaking.
Questions answered

Frequently asked

Presenting “Project Members” solo?
List yourself as sole member and author. You may mention hypothetical stakeholders or the client.
A control that does not fit one type or function?
Controls overlap. Choose the primary type and function and justify briefly. A guard is physical and can be preventive or detective.
Must the PowerPoint be finished?
No, begin it. At minimum draft the Introduction and the RMF (risk matrix) sections.
Most common Pre-Defense mistake?
Getting too technical and forgetting the business context. Focus on why the risk matters, what the control does and how it protects assets.
