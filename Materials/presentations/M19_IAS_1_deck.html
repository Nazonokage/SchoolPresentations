Information Assurance and Security · Module 19

RMF steps 5 and 6: authorization and monitoring

Executives decide; monitoring never stops.

Preview and review

Finishing the framework

Quick review
  • Step 5: authorize information systems
  • Step 6: monitor security controls
Roles
Step 5 is mostly for executives or owners, who give final approval. Step 6 improves on Step 4 so controls stay effective over time.
Step 5

Authorize information systems

Who and how
Not done by security officers. Executives or owners authorize; officers create the documentation so they can decide with information.
Executives must
  • Accept or decline the provided controls
  • Assign an employee to handle and observe each risk and control
  • Accept the costs of implementing the controls
  • Accept the damages if a control fails or was declined

Authorization keeps owners aware of their security state, costs and budget. They have the final say on the treatment: prevent, reduce, transfer or accept. It bridges a recommendation and an implemented control; without sign-off nothing is funded.

Step 6

Monitor security controls

Continuous
Improves on the assess step. The risk and selected controls are monitored unless something changes or needs changing.
Triggers for change
  • The risk is no longer highly severe, so its cost should be reduced.
  • A new technology is introduced, so a new control will be implemented.

After step 6, the process loops back to step 1: categorization. When monitoring shows changes, repeat the process so no detail of the risk is missed.

Analogy and use case

Home security and a retail POS

Home security
Step 5: you review alarm quotes, pick a package, sign and pay, and accept the risk of losing your TV if it fails. Step 6: two years on, a safer neighborhood lets you downgrade monitoring, or a new smart lock triggers a new assessment, back to step 1.
Retail point-of-sale
The officer recommends an encrypted POS; the owner approves the budget and assigns the Store Manager. Six months later the manager reports a higher license fee; the owner switches vendors, triggering a full reassessment.
Course project

Conclusion chapter

Two components
  • Authorized information systems: your recommendations are decided by the owner and executives, who may apply whichever they choose.
  • Monitor security controls: whether they accept or reject your recommendations, they must assign people to monitor risks and controls.
Example
The recommendations are subject to final approval of the owners and executives. Implementation, costs and residual risks rest with them. Regardless of the decision, the organization must assign dedicated personnel to continuously monitor its risks and controls.
Questions answered

Frequently asked

How important is authorization?
Very. Without it there is no budget, accountability or implementation.
Who monitors?
Often a specific employee or manager in the department that owns the asset, as assigned during authorization.
What triggers a loop to step 1?
A significant change: new threats, a new technology or asset, or a change in budget or risk appetite.
Executive declines a control?
They are accepting the risk, and the organization (possibly the executive personally) bears the full damage.
