Network auditing and traffic analysis

Understanding what is on the network

Nmap (Network Mapper)
Audits of various OS-based network systems. One of the oldest information security tools, still updated yearly. Helps with any network-based IT investigation with the fastest results possible.
Wireshark
Detailed information about what is happening on your network. Decryption support for many protocols; export to XML, PostScript, CSV or plain text.
Snort
Open-source platform for network intrusion prevention. Supports Windows and can watch network packets and stream data to your screen.

Passwords and antivirus

John the Ripper and Bitdefender

John the Ripper
The most promising tool for password cracking and testing the strength of passwords and credentials. Uses brute force; mainly used by developers to test their password security. Open source for Linux, Windows, Mac and Android.
Bitdefender Total Security
All-around antivirus and security software for commercial and personal use. A solid choice for viruses or firewall attacks, though pricey compared with free options.
Linux and Kali Linux

The operating system for the toolbox

Linux
Considered the most secure operating system in existence, because it handles .exe files differently and needs a separate process to permit execution, so viruses cannot easily auto-run.
Why Kali Linux?
  • Pre-installed tools for penetration, forensics and security work.
  • Comprehensive: track, deploy, configure and search for weaknesses in networks, audits and operating systems.
  • Ethical hacking and penetration testing; highly customizable.

Analogy and use case

Mechanic’s toolbox and a penetration test

Mechanic’s toolbox
Nmap and Wireshark are diagnostic scanners. John the Ripper is a lock-picking set to test car doors. Nessus and Netsparker are safety inspectors looking for rust. Kali Linux is the rolling toolbox holding them all.
Penetration test
A hired consultant boots Kali Linux; uses Nmap to find open ports; Nessus or Netsparker to scan them for known flaws like SQL injection; John the Ripper on hashed passwords; Wireshark to check for unencrypted sensitive traffic.

Questions answered

Frequently asked

More tools?
Yes. These are the most common. Many organizations also build tailor-made tools for their infrastructure.
Why do developers use John the Ripper?
To test their own passwords by brute force and enforce stronger policies before hackers exploit weak ones.
Kali vs regular Linux?
Kali is a specialized distribution with hundreds of pre-installed penetration testing and auditing tools.
Is Bitdefender better than free antivirus?
It gives comprehensive protection with firewall and advanced threat detection. The choice depends on budget and risk appetite.
