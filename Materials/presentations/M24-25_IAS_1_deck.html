Information Assurance and Security · Module 24-25

Final project update and defense preparation

A workshop: no new theory, just your polished report and presentation.

Preview

The final stretch

This module
A dedicated project workshop. Synthesize risk identification, the RMF, security policies, user access controls and external services into one report and presentation.
Solo version
You are the sole independent security consultant, presenting your audit to the “executives” (your instructor).
Completion guidelines 1

RMF steps 3 and 4

Step 3: Recommendations
A chapter titled “Recommendations.” Give a detailed recommendation for every risk identified earlier, structured with Module 18.
Step 4: Recommended Assessment
A subsection of Recommendations titled “Recommended Assessment.” Recommend assessment for every recommendation, risk and control, and state the 3 assessment questions for future assessors.
Completion guidelines 2

Steps 5 and 6, and enrichment

Step 5: Conclusion
A “Conclusion” chapter on the authorization step: the organization is free to apply your recommendations, but executives or owners must note the action. Stress the importance of an IAS document.
Step 6: Monitoring
In the Conclusion, a paragraph on why someone should monitor risks and controls, whether or not recommendations were accepted.
Enrichment
Add risks and benefits from the Fortuitous Events module. Recommendations include specific software, tools and external services (law enforcement, emergency services, legal entities).

Final pitch to the board

After weeks auditing, you walk into the boardroom with your report and slide deck. Show the problems, and confidently articulate the solutions and the business value.

The introduction sets scope; the RMF and matrix prove due diligence; recommendations and policies are actionable; the conclusion is your sign-off: the decision, and the liability, rests with executives.

Questions answered

Frequently asked

Not ready to defend?
The two-day window gives time. Defend if ready; otherwise polish and practice.
Many risks in Recommendations?
Use subheadings or a table: control (type and function) and why it mitigates the risk.
Why monitoring if they reject recommendations?
Monitoring is continuous; even if they accept a risk, they must know if it materializes.
A question you cannot answer?
Be honest: say you will look into it, or reason from the frameworks. Knowing your limits is professional.
How detailed is “About the organization”?
Enough context for the risks: employees, what they sell, how they handle data.
