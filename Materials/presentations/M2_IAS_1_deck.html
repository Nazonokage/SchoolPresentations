Information Assurance and Security 1 · Module 2

Introduction to Information Assurance and Security

Technology is woven into daily life. Your job is to secure it so routines stay undisturbed.

Learning targets

By the end of this module you can

  • Describe information assurance and security, and threats.
  • Identify different kinds of security threats.
  • Discuss the importance of knowing security threats.
  • Discuss the preliminaries about the project for the subject.

Constant emergence of cyber vulnerabilities demands robust countermeasures.

Core concept

What is information assurance?

Definition
The set of measures intended to protect and defend information and information systems by ensuring their availability, integrity, authentication, confidentiality and non-repudiation.
It is the practice of
  • Assuring information.
  • Managing risks related to the use, processing, storage and transmission of information or data.
  • Protecting the systems and processes used for those purposes.
Why it matters

From computer security to information assurance

Computer security
Focused on stand-alone computers. It could not safeguard data during processing, storing or transmission across systems.
Information security
Expanded the mission of “information protection.”
Information assurance
Safeguards information in intricate or scattered networks and uses dynamic protection that can identify assaults and enable a defense.

Pro tip: IA covers privacy, regulatory compliance, audits, business continuity and disaster recovery. It is a superset of information security.

The IA process

Six steps to total risk

  1. 1. Enumeration and classification: identify and classify the information assets to protect.
  2. 2. Risk assessment: perform a risk assessment for those assets.
  3. 3. Vulnerability identification: find vulnerabilities and the threats able to exploit them.
  4. 4. Probability assessment: how likely is a threat to exploit a vulnerability?
  5. 5. Impact assessment: usually measured as cost to the stakeholders.
  6. 6. Total risk: the sum of the products of each threat’s impact and probability.

Strategy and comparison

Information assurance strategy and InfoSec

Multi-layered IA strategy
  1. Cyber security awareness and education.
  2. Strong cryptography.
  3. Good security-enabled commercial information technology.
  4. An enabling global Security Management Infrastructure.
  5. A civil defense infrastructure with attack sensing, warning capability and a coordinated response mechanism.
Information security (InfoSec)
Defending information from unauthorized access, use, disclosure, disruption, modification, perusal, inspection, recording or destruction. Two major aspects: IT security (computer security), applied to technology; and information assurance, ensuring data is not lost when critical issues arise, such as natural disasters, server malfunction or physical theft.
Understanding threats

What is a threat?

Definition
A malicious act that aims to corrupt or steal data, or disrupt an organization’s systems or the entire organization.
Why it matters
Threats range from insider threats to advanced persistent threats. If the internal security team is not informed and prepared, these risks could bring the organization to its knees.
Top 10 threats (2022 landscape)

Threats 1 to 3

1. Social engineering
Focuses on interpersonal interactions; persuades people to disregard security norms to gain illegal access. 85% of all data breaches involve human interaction (Verizon DBIR).
2. Third-party exposure
Damages alleged by clients or third parties for which the firm may be liable. A 2021 breach at contractor Socialarks leaked data from over 214 million Facebook, Instagram and LinkedIn accounts.
3. Configuration mistakes
Even very effective systems almost always have an installation or configuration flaw. Rapid7 found vulnerable misconfigurations in 80% of external penetration tests, 96% with inside access.
Top 10 threats

Threats 4 to 6

4. Poor cyber hygiene
Routines such as avoiding open WiFi and using a VPN or MFA. Nearly 60% of organizations rely on human memory for passwords, 42% use sticky notes, 54% of IT professionals do not require MFA.
5. Cloud vulnerabilities
Up 150% over the past five years (IBM). Over 90% of the 29,000 breaches in Verizon’s DBIR were due to web app breaches.
6. Mobile device vulnerabilities
COVID-19 raised mobile use, remote work and BYOD policies. 46% of companies had an incident involving a malicious mobile app downloaded by an employee (Check Point).
Top 10 threats

Threats 7 and 8

7. Internet of Things (IoT)
Interrelated computing devices, machines, objects, animals or people with unique identifiers that transfer data over a network without human-to-human or human-to-computer interaction. Over 1.5 billion breaches occurred between January and June of 2021.
8. Ransomware
A subset of malware that locks data using encryption and demands payment before unlocking. 66% of companies suffered significant revenue loss and 29% were forced to remove jobs (Cybereason).
Top 10 threats

Threats 9 and 10

9. Poor data management
Consumer-generated data doubles every four years, but more than half is never used or evaluated. In 2018, Aetna was ordered to pay $17 million after mailing sensitive health information in the wrong type of envelope.
10. Inadequate post-attack procedures
Flaws must be fixed right away. 80% of victims that paid the ransom quickly came under another attack. 60% of cyber attacks could have been avoided if a patch had been issued (Cybereason).
Analogy

The smart home

Pillars at home
  • Availability: electricity and internet stay on.
  • Integrity: no one breaks your doors or messes with smart locks.
  • Confidentiality: curtains drawn so outsiders cannot see in.
  • Authentication: fingerprint or keycode unlocks the door.
  • Non-repudiation: camera footage proves who entered and left.
Social engineering
A burglar pretends to be a delivery driver and tricks you into opening the door. That exploits human trust rather than breaking the lock.
Real-world use cases

Healthcare and e-commerce

Healthcare
A hospital protects patient records (confidentiality) while doctors reach life-saving data instantly (availability). A ransomware attack exploits poor post-attack procedures and causes massive disruption.
E-commerce
An online store safeguards credit card data with strong cryptography and PCI DSS compliance. A third-party exposure risks reputational damage and regulatory fines.

Recap

Key takeaways

  • IA goes beyond securing data: risk management, business continuity and disaster recovery.
  • The five pillars: availability, integrity, authentication, confidentiality, non-repudiation.
  • The IA process is a cyclical risk model from asset enumeration to total risk.
  • Human error (social engineering, poor cyber hygiene) sits behind most breaches (85%).
  • Threats evolve: patch promptly and build security awareness.
Activity 1

Identification

  1. _____ covers protecting against improper information modification or damage, and includes ensuring non-repudiation and authenticity.
  2. _____ is considered malicious acts, to corrupt or steal data or disrupt an organization’s systems or the entire organization.
  3. _____ is to preserve authorized restrictions on access and disclosure, including protecting personal privacy and proprietary information.
  1. _____ is the set of measures intended to protect and defend information and information systems by ensuring their availability, integrity, authentication, confidentiality and non-repudiation.
  2. _____ is to ensure timely and reliable access to and use of information.
Activity 2

Check for understanding

  • In any organization, why do we need IAS?
  • Explain social engineering and give an example based on your understanding or experience.
Answer key

Activity 1

  1. Integrity: protects against improper modification.
  2. Threat: a malicious act against data or systems.
  3. Confidentiality: restricts access and disclosure.
  1. Information assurance: the full set of protective measures across the five pillars.
  2. Availability: timely, reliable access.
Answer key

Activity 2

  • 1. Discuss reliance on technology, protecting data from unauthorized access, business continuity (availability), and managing evolving vulnerabilities to prevent financial loss, reputational damage and disruption.
  • 2. A non-technical attack that uses human interaction and psychological manipulation to make people break security protocols. Examples: phishing emails, pretexting (posing as IT support to get a password), baiting (leaving an infected USB drive in a parking lot).
