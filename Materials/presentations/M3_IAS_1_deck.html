Legalities

Four laws to follow

Copyright Law
Protects original works of authorship (documents, code, media). If another group copies your project without permission, it violates copyright law.
Financial Reporting (Serbanes-Oxley Act)
Requires accurate financial reporting. Failure to report properly can lead to heavy fines and even jail time.
Digital Millennium Copyright Act (DMCA)
A specialized copyright law for digital media and software. It protects the lines of code you write, the systems you build and project documentation.
Data Privacy Act
Protects the privacy of personal data. We discuss it in detail later in this module.

Laws are rules that mandate or prohibit certain societal behaviors. In short, they stop people from doing bad things.

Ethics

The Ten Commandments of Computer Ethics

  1. Thou shalt not use a computer to harm other people.
  2. Thou shalt not interfere with other people’s computer work.
  3. Thou shalt not snoop around in other people’s computer files.
  4. Thou shalt not use a computer to steal.
  5. Thou shalt not use a computer to bear false witness.
  1. Thou shalt not copy or use proprietary software for which you have not paid (without permission).
  2. Thou shalt not use other people’s computer resources without authorization or proper compensation.
  3. Thou shalt not appropriate other people’s intellectual output.
  4. Thou shalt think about the social consequences of the program you are writing or the system you are designing.
  5. Thou shalt always use a computer in ways that ensure consideration and respect for other humans.

Ethics define socially acceptable behavior.

Implications and punishments

Laws carry sanctions, while ethics do not

DomainType of violationPotential consequences
ProfessionalFailing to follow organizational rules and policies.Suspension, salary reduction, mandatory retraining, or termination (depending on severity).
LegalViolating enacted laws (Copyright, DMCA, SOX, Data Privacy).Fines, community service, and imprisonment (depending on the severity of the violated law).
EthicalViolating social norms and ethical standards.Damage to social status, loss of trust, avoidance by peers, severe reputation damage, and difficulty getting hired.
The CIA Triad

Three core components of information security

Confidentiality
Information is accessible only to those authorized. Example: only you can access your university email or personal accounts.
Integrity
Accuracy and completeness of information and processing methods. Example: a memo about a canceled class has the correct date, time and the signature of the officer who declared it.
Availability
Authorized users have access when required. Example: only university students can access the student portal, and they can recover forgotten passwords.
Data Privacy Act

Republic Act No. 10173

What it does
Seeks to protect all forms of information, whether private, personal or sensitive. It covers both natural and juridical persons involved in the processing of personal information.
Where you have seen it
When you fill out a digital form such as Google Forms, the first part usually asks for your consent to the Data Privacy Act. This tells you the information you write is legally protected and secured.
Analogy

The security guard and the rulebook

Legal issues = police and government laws
If you steal from the building, you go to jail.
Professional issues = security guard and HR policies
If you break the building’s rules, you get fired or suspended.
Ethical issues = the social contract among tenants
If you are rude, snoop on neighbors or act unprofessionally, people avoid you and you lose your reputation.
Real-world use cases

Three scenarios

Software development and DMCA
A developer copies a proprietary algorithm from a competitor’s software without paying. This violates the DMCA and Copyright Law, leading to massive lawsuits and financial damages.
Data privacy in healthcare
A hospital administrator emails a spreadsheet of patient names and diagnoses to the wrong mailing list. A severe Data Privacy Act violation: heavy fines, mandatory breach notifications, lost public trust.
Financial reporting (SOX)
An IT manager is asked to alter database logs to hide a financial discrepancy. Complying violates the Serbanes-Oxley Act (SOX), with personal criminal liability and jail time.

Course project

Data privacy and compliance audit

Guidelines
  • Group of 3–5 members
  • Evaluate compliance with Copyright, DMCA and the Data Privacy Act
  • Assess the ethical implications of data handling
  • Recommend improvements
  • Sample title: A Compliance and Data Privacy Audit of a Local E-Commerce Startup
  • This is distinct from the previous module’s security assessment
Deliverable for Module 3
  1. Select a target (real or hypothetical).
  2. Identify legal gaps: checklist of the 4 laws. Is a Privacy Policy displayed? Is software licensed?
  3. Evaluate ethical practices: undisclosed uses of data? Respect for consent?
  4. Draft a 1-page code of conduct for the IT department, adapting the Ten Commandments.
  5. Present findings: legal and ethical risks.

Recap

Key takeaways

  • Legalities, ethics and professionalism are three distinct but interconnected pillars.
  • Laws carry legal sanctions (fines, jail time); ethics carry social sanctions (lost reputation and trust).
  • The CIA Triad is the core framework for securing information.
  • The Data Privacy Act (RA 10173) mandates protection of personal data and requires consent.
  • You are responsible for knowing and enforcing these standards through training, awareness and policy compliance.
Activity 1

Identification

  1. _____ is the law that requires organizations to accurately report their financial information, with penalties including fines and jail time.
  2. _____ defines socially acceptable behavior but does not carry legal sanctions.
  3. _____ ensures that information is accessible only to those authorized to have access.
  1. _____ is the Philippine law (RA 10173) that seeks to protect all forms of personal information.
  2. _____ is a subset of copyright law that focuses specifically on digital media, software code and documentation.
Activity 2

Check for understanding

  • The CIA Triad stands for what?
  • In developing systems, at what part of the development process do we need to enforce copyright and DMCA, and why?
  • Compare and contrast the consequences of violating a law versus violating an ethical standard in the IT profession.
Answer key

Activity 1

  1. Serbanes-Oxley Act (SOX): it mandates accurate financial reporting.
  2. Ethics: social norms without legal sanctions.
  3. Confidentiality: access only for the authorized.
  1. Data Privacy Act: RA 10173 protects personal information.
  2. Digital Millennium Copyright Act (DMCA): copyright for digital media and code.
Answer key

Activity 2

  • 1. Confidentiality, Integrity and Availability.
  • 2. During the entire SDLC, most crucially at Requirements Gathering and Design/Implementation. Requirements must not infringe existing patents or copyrights; developers must not copy proprietary code without a license. Early compliance prevents costly legal battles, fines and rewrites.
  • 3. Laws bring formal government sanctions: fines, imprisonment, community service. Ethics brings social and professional sanctions: termination, lost reputation, difficulty finding work, ostracization. The state enforces laws; the community and one’s moral compass enforce ethics.
