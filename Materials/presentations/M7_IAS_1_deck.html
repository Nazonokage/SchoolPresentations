Information Assurance and Security · Module 7

First project update: your risk management plan

Combine Modules 2 to 6 into one project document, as a solo security consultant.

Preview and review

Putting the pieces together

Quick review
  • Legal and ethical: risks from breaking laws (Data Privacy Act) or professional ethics.
  • Risk identification: brainstorm risks; rate likelihood and severity.
  • RMF step 1: categorize and prioritize with your risk matrix.
Important note
Originally a group activity, now a solo project. You act as an independent security consultant for a chosen organization or business. No new concepts: you apply what you have learned.
The checklist

Five elements of the update

  1. Legal, ethical and professional issues: risks from mishandling customer data (Data Privacy) or copying software without a license (Copyright).
  2. Risk identification and assessment: brainstorm every risk, interview the owner or research the industry; rate likelihood and severity from 1 to 5.
  3. RMF table: finalize the layout listing all identified risks.
  1. Risk matrix: a custom matrix that fits the organization’s risk appetite.
  2. Applying RMF step 1: use your matrix to set each risk’s priority level and add it as a column in the RMF table.
Deliverables and analogy

The architect’s blueprint

Deliverable
One comprehensive document (or set) with the initial risk assessment of your target. It is the foundation for selecting and implementing controls later.
Analogy
You learned materials (legal and ethics), structural stresses (risk identification) and building codes (RMF). Now you draw the first draft of the blueprint: not building yet, but putting measurements and priorities on one page so the team knows where to start.
Use case

The local bookstore

Risks
  • Legal/ethical: customer names and emails on a spreadsheet violate the Data Privacy Act if not secured.
  • Identification: flooding (physical), point-of-sale hacking (digital), employee theft (internal).
Matrix and output
The owner has a moderate risk appetite: a little downtime is tolerable, but losing customer data is catastrophic. “Most Important” risks affect data integrity. The output is a table of risks, likelihood, impact and priority.

Project steps 1 to 3

Choose, find legal risks, list risks

Steps
  • 1. Choose: a small business, fictional company or university department (example: “The Cozy Nook Bookstore”).
  • 2. Legal/ethical risks: write 2 specific to it (for example, mishandling card data violates PCI DSS; unlicensed website images violate DMCA).
  • 3. Identify 5-8 risks with likelihood and severity from 1 to 5.

Project steps 4 to 6

Matrix, categorize, reflect

Steps
  • 4. Custom matrix: draw a 5x5 grid and shade “Most Important” boxes differently from “Least Important”, based on risk appetite.
  • 5. Categorize (RMF step 1): add a Priority Level column from your matrix.
  • 6. Reflect: Notable Changes (surprising risks found) and Concerns (hardest part of the solo project).

Questions answered

Frequently asked

Working solo, how do I get information?
Use a hypothetical or publicly known small business (a local cafe, an online freelancer). Research common industry risks and simulate their risk appetite by size and sector.
How many risks?
At least 5-8 distinct risks, enough to show likelihood, severity and prioritization.
Notable Changes and Concerns?
Reflection questions. Changes: pivots from your research. Concerns: challenges, such as a reluctant owner or hard impact estimates.
Need a Google Drive folder?
Keep an organized digital portfolio (computer or cloud folder) with all files from Modules 2 to 7.

  1. B: risk appetite drives prioritization.
