Information Assurance and Security · Module 8

Attack and defense methods

Understand how adversaries act so you can build better defenses. No actual attacks in this module.

Learning targets

By the end you can

  • Differentiate attacks from threats.
  • Describe different types of IAS attacks.
  • Describe how to defend against IAS attacks.
Threats vs attacks

Distinct meanings

Threat
A category of objects, persons or other entities that presents a danger to an asset. Always present; purposeful or undirected. Hackers purposely threaten unprotected systems; severe storms incidentally threaten buildings.
Attack
An intentional or unintentional act that can cause damage to or otherwise compromise information and/or the systems that support it.
Classifying attacks

Three ways to classify

ClassificationDescriptionExamples
Active vs passiveActive: directly changing sensitive information. Passive: casually reading or observing it.Active: changing a grade in a school database. Passive: shoulder surfing to read a password.
Intentional vs unintentionalIntentional: a deliberate malicious act. Unintentional: accidents or natural events.Hacker break-in. Lightning causing a server room fire.
Direct vs indirectDirect: originates from the threat itself. Indirect: originates from a compromised system working under a threat’s control.Direct: a hacker’s own computer. Indirect: a botnet.
Intentional attacks 1

Passwords, XSS and IoT

Password attack
Trying to access an account or device by determining the password: social engineering, hacking a password database, guessing, brute force, or inputting all possible passwords.
Cross-site scripting (XSS)
Malicious scripts placed in reliable websites, mainly in the HTML. A victim clicks a script-injected link, the browser runs the script, and private data goes to the attacker.
IoT attacks
Exploit the convenience of interconnected devices. Example: linking a bank account to an e-commerce platform creates a vulnerability if the platform is breached.
Intentional attacks 2

Malware, phishing and zero-day

Malware
Viruses, worms and trojans. The most common IAS threat and the main reason for anti-virus software.
Phishing
A scam: millions of emails or texts, often asking users to update a profile on a website, capturing their credentials.
Zero-day exploit
Occurs with new software. Untested against hackers, it is easily attacked before a patch is released.
Unintentional threats

Not all threats are malicious

  1. Coding errors: for example, forgetting important validation.
  2. Admin errors: administrators or users accidentally modify data.
  3. Natural disasters: storms, earthquakes, floods.
  4. Power supply loss: brownouts or blackouts.
  1. ISP issues: faulty telecommunications.
  2. Peripheral devices: a monitor or phone seen in peripheral view (shoulder surfing) exposes passwords.
  3. Server issues: errors that make servers malfunction or stop.
Database and network attacks

SQL injection, DoS and MitM

SQL injection
Entering “invalid” information into software that uses SQL, so attackers can manipulate or destroy the data. More in IAS 2.
Denial-of-service (DoS)
Attackers flood the system, server or network so it fails from overprocessing requests, causing data loss and stopped operations.
Man-in-the-middle (MitM)
An attacker intercepts communications between devices, sitting in between (such as on a router or switch) to receive and send data.
Defenses

Against intentional attacks

DefenseWhat it does
Intrusion detection system (IDS)Monitors network and system activity; can stop attacks automatically or warn officers. Used against DoS and MitM.
Information security management system (ISMS)Like an IDS at larger scale; tracks employee activity and can create risk assessments.
Anti-virusPrevents and removes malware.
FirewallsCrucial to a network; prevent malicious hackers from accessing the network or devices.
Software updatesPatches remove faults, removing zero-day exploits.
SQL injection defenseLearn SQL injection and how to protect against it (IAS 2).
Defenses

Against unintentional attacks

Training and awareness
Simulations, exercises or seminars on protecting accounts. Also lessens admin errors and peripheral device issues.
Natural disasters
Cannot be avoided, but planning and safety measures can reduce damage to almost negligible: fire drills, training, proper equipment placement, electrical machines on a higher floor.
Power, ISP and server loss
Generators for power, a backup ISP if available, proper server use with regular backups.

Analogy and use case

Vault and coffee shop

High-security vault
Threat: skilled robbers (intentional) or an earthquake (unintentional). Attack: drilling the lock, or an employee leaving the door open. Defense: armed guards (IDS), steel walls (firewalls), earthquake-proof foundations (disaster planning).
Coffee shop public Wi-Fi
A MitM attacker sets up a fake hotspot, “CoffeeShop_Free_WiFi.” Defenses: a firewall and WPA3 encryption on the real network, signs advising customers to use a VPN, and baristas trained to spot people tampering with the router.

Questions answered

Frequently asked

Threat vs attack?
A threat is the source of danger (a hacker, a storm). An attack is the action that exploits a vulnerability (breaking in, flooding a server room).
Why consider unintentional attacks?
They can do as much damage as intentional ones and are often overlooked.
Firewall vs IDS?
A firewall is a guard at the door blocking traffic by rules. An IDS is a security camera monitoring and alerting.
Can we stop all attacks?
No. The goal is to manage risk and recover quickly.
Key quotes

Definitions worth remembering

Threat
A category of entities that presents a danger to an asset.
Attack
An act that can cause damage to or compromise information or its systems.
Zero-day exploit
A newly discovered vulnerability the vendor has not yet patched.
Goal of IA
Not perfect security, but to manage risk and recover quickly.
Recap

Key takeaways

  • Threats are potential dangers; attacks are the acts that cause harm.
  • Attacks are active or passive, intentional or unintentional, direct or indirect.
  • Common intentional attacks: malware, phishing, SQL injection, XSS, DoS.
  • Unintentional threats: natural disasters, power loss, human error.
  • Strong defense mixes technical, administrative and physical controls.
